1Getting in

Go to cmtaylorstory.com/admin and log in with your email address and password. That’s the whole thing – the same address every time, and you can bookmark it.

You’ll see three sections down the left: Films, Books and About page. That’s everything you can change.

2Nothing goes live until you say so

This is the part worth knowing before anything else, because it means you can poke about without worrying.

When you save something, it becomes a draft. The website does not change. You can leave it half-finished, come back tomorrow, change your mind entirely, or throw it away.

Only when you press Publish does the site actually update, and that takes a minute or two to appear.

Changed your mind?

Open the draft and choose Delete unpublished entry. The website never saw it.

3Adding a film

  1. Click Films, then + Film.
  2. Fill in what you know. Only the name, the year, the length and the one-line description are needed – everything else can wait.
  3. Press Save. It’s a draft.
  4. When you’re happy, press Publish.

As you type, the right-hand side shows the actual page being built. That is what it will look like.

The one that catches people out

Link to watch it – leave this empty while the film is still going round festivals, and the poster shows instead. Paste the Vimeo link in when it’s ready to watch, and the film appears in its place. That single field is what changes the film from “on the festival circuit” to “watch the film” on the Films page.

Festival awards

Add one row per award and upload the laurel picture, exactly as the festival sent it – no preparing needed. They are all turned white and sized to match on the page automatically. The one thing to send to Jack instead is a laurel that comes out as a solid white block: that means it arrived without a transparent background.

4Adding a book

  1. Click Books, then + Book.
  2. Title, year, and the one line that sits under the title.
  3. What the book is about takes one box per paragraph. Press Add for another. Use the I button for italics rather than typing anything special.
  4. Add any review quotes and shops, one row each.
  5. Save, then Publish when you’re ready.
The cover is the exception

Your jackets are cut from your own paintings, so a new one needs making up before it will look right. Add the book with everything else, and send Jack the artwork – he’ll drop it in.

5Changing your About page

Click Pages, then About page.

The writing is broken into labelled pieces rather than one long block, so you can change one part without hunting through the rest: the opening line, your story, the line about teaching, your courses, the editing paragraph, the personal bit and the Substack line.

Each box has the same B and I buttons for bold and italics. Type normally.

Your courses

One box per course. Press Add for another, or the × to remove one that’s finished.

6Changing your Contact page

Click Pages, then Contact page.

You can change the heading and the words above the form – one box per paragraph, so an agent’s details can sit in their own paragraph. Type an email address as it is and it turns into a link on the page.

The thank-you message people see after sending is there too.

The form itself – the name, email and message boxes – can’t be changed here. Messages sent through it arrive by email as before.

7Pictures

Wherever you see an image box, click it and choose a file from your computer.

Two kinds of picture are better sent to Jack than uploaded:

  • Book covers – cut from your paintings, so they need making up to match the others.
  • Festival laurels that show as a solid white block – those arrived without a transparent background and need cutting out. Every other laurel is fine to upload yourself.

Film posters are fine to upload yourself.

8What updates by itself

The Essays page. It reads your Substack directly. Publish a post there and it appears on your site on its own – there is nothing to do here, and nothing to keep in step.

The homepage films. The clip behind your name changes on each visit, working through them in turn.

Messages. Anything sent through the contact form comes straight to you by email.

9Seeing how many people visit

There is a Visitor stats button in the bottom right of the editor. It opens a page showing how many people have been to the site, which pages they read, where in the world they were, and which site sent them – useful for telling whether a Substack post actually brought anyone over.

You do not need to log in. It is a read-only page: nothing there can change the site, so it is safe to bookmark or pass to anyone you like.

It counts from the day it was switched on, not before. The numbers from the old WordPress site could not come across – they belonged to WordPress rather than to the site itself.

Nobody is identified. It records that a visit happened and roughly where from, not who. There are no cookies and nothing follows anyone around, which is why the site needs no “accept cookies” box.

10If something looks wrong

Nothing you do here is permanent until you publish, and even after that everything is recoverable – every version is kept, so anything can be put back.

  • You published something by mistake. Tell Jack. It can be undone.
  • A change hasn’t appeared. Give it two or three minutes, then refresh the page.
  • A picture looks wrong on the page. Usually the image itself rather than anything you did. Send it over.
  • You can’t log in. Use the forgotten-password link on the login screen.
